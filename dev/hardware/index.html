Hardware¶

The TMC5160A's package, pins, bus settings, timing and power, as the driver uses them.

Datasheet sections this page relies on

From Analog Devices (Trinamic) TMC5160A-DS 1.18:

  • Package, pins and signal descriptions: 2 Pin Assignments, p. 11
  • Sample circuits and gate drive: 3 Sample Circuits, p. 15
  • Absolute maximum ratings and operational range: 28.1 Operational Range, p. 120
  • DC and timing characteristics: 28.2 DC and Timing Characteristics, p. 121
  • Thermal characteristics: 28.3 Thermal Characteristics, p. 124
  • Package mechanical data and codes: 30 Package Mechanical Data, p. 128

All of them, with the driver API for each: Datasheet map.

The driver drives only DRV_ENN of the control pins. CtrlPin::DcEnable and CtrlPin::DcIn exist for a port that wires them, InputPin::DcOut likewise: no call of the driver sets or reads them.

Package and pins¶

The TMC5160 and TMC5160A are sold in a 48-pin TQFP with exposed pad (7 x 7 mm, 0.5 mm pitch) and in a wettable-flank QFN (8 x 8 mm). The driver uses the same registers on both.

Figure description and sources

The TMC5160A seen from the top, in its TQFP-48 package. Pin 1 is marked by the dot; the numbers run counter-clockwise from it. Each pad is coloured by the pin's electrical type (legend below). Next to it is the pin's name and, in mono, the hook of the driver's port that drives or reads it; a pin without a hook is not used by the driver. The dashed square is the exposed pad.

Operating modes and straps¶

Mode SD_MODE SPI_MODE Interface Driver
Motion controller low high (SPI) or low (UART) SPI or UART Mode::MotionController
STEP/DIR with serial configuration high high SPI Mode::StepDir
Standalone STEP/DIR, configured by the CFG pins high low none not drivable

EnsureInitialized() reads IOIN and compares the SD_MODE level with Config::mode; a mismatch returns ModeMismatch.

Control pins¶

The driver drives DRV_ENN, and no other control pin, through the optional GpioSet() hook, with logical levels: GpioSignal::ACTIVE means "driver enabled", which your port writes as a low level on DRV_ENN. A board that wires DRV_ENN low permanently leaves the hook out: Enable() then writes TOFF only, and Disable() writes TOFF 0. Configure the pin with the driver off before it becomes an output.

DIAG0 and DIAG1 are read with GpioGet(). They are open drain by default and need a pull-up of 47 kOhm or less.

Supplies¶

Rail Range Source
VS 10 V to 55 V Motor and bridge supply (section 28.1, page 120)
VSA 10 V to 50 V Analog supply for the 11.5 V and 5 V regulators
VCC_IO 3.0 V to 5.25 V I/O supply, sets the logic levels

The cover page says 8 V to 60 V; the driver uses the operating range of page 120 (Ambiguities, A50). The slopes of VS at power-on and power-off must stay below 1 V per microsecond, and ripple above 0.5 V endangers the charge pump (pages 15 and 16).

Clock¶

The internal oscillator runs at about 12 MHz (practical tolerance 4 percent); an external clock of 4 MHz to 18 MHz on CLK is accepted (10 to 16 MHz advised). Every time, velocity and acceleration value scales with the clock, so declare it in Config::clock.

Bus settings¶

Setting SPI UART
Mode Mode 3: SCK idle high, sampled on the rising edge 8N1, LSB first
Clock Up to 4 MHz on the internal clock, fCLK / 2 less a 10 percent margin on an external one 9000 baud to fCLK / 16, set from the sync nibble
Frame 40 bits, CSN low for the whole frame 8 bytes write, 4 bytes read request, 8 bytes reply
Integrity none CRC-8

SPI timing (section 4.3, page 25): CSN high time more than 2 tCLK + 10 ns, SCK high and low more than tCLK + 10 ns, SDI setup and hold 10 ns. The driver does not time the bus: the port's clock sets it.

Timing the driver waits for¶

Parameter Value Driver
Interface idle after a UART error 12 bit times kUartRecoveryBits
StealthChop automatic tuning AT#1 up to 130 ms SetStealthChop() waits with bounded DelayUs() calls
DRV_ENN switch-off 3 clock cycles Disable()

Reset¶

A chip reset (undervoltage on VSA, 5VOUT or VCC_IO, or power-up) sets every register to its reset value. VCC is not monitored (section 25, page 119). After a microcontroller reboot the datasheet asks: TOFF 0, rewrite all registers, stop the ramp. EnsureInitialized() does exactly that; the driver never relies on the chip's registers surviving.
